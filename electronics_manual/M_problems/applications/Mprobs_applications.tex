\begin{Exercise}
\label{prob_jfet_as_resistor}
When you used an electret microphone in the lab, you connected the microphone in series with a $5.6\kohm$ resistor as shown below.  
Suppose that in the circuit shown, the voltage at the output of the microphone capsule has a DC level of 3~volts and an AC level of 80~mV peak-to-peak.   

\figuretoright{0.39}{M_problems/applications/microphone_circuit.pdf}{
\begin{enumerate}[nosep, label=(\alph*)]
\item Making the gross over-simplification that the JFET inside the microphone is behaving like a variable resistor, determine the apparent range of the JFET's resistance in this circuit.  
\item Assuming the JFET's effective range of resistance is unchanged from the previous part, what would be the AC output level if you used an external resistance of $R=1\kohm$?  
\item What would be the AC output level if you used an external resistance of $R=100\kohm$?  
\item Write a rule of thumb for how to choose the value of external resistance to use in a JFET circuit like this one to maximize the AC output.
\end{enumerate}
}

\end{Exercise}
\begin{Answer}
(a) $8.125\kohm$ to $8.686\kohm$ (b) 31.7 mV peak-to-peak (c) 23.9 mV peak-to-peak.
\end{Answer}

Suppose again that the voltage at the output of the microphone capsule has a DC level of 3~volts.
\begin{enumerate}[nosep, label=(\alph*)]

\item From the graph below, what is the value of $V_\text{GS}$?
\item From the graph, estimate the value of $\partial I_D / \partial V_\text{GS}$ at the values of $V_\text{DS}$ and $V_\text{GS}$ you found above.
\item If the microphone capsule output has an AC voltage of 80~mV peak-to-peak, what is the AC voltage of $V_\text{GS}$?
\item The AC voltage $\Delta V_\text{GS}$ is a result of the vibrating electret element, and the microphone circuit is acting like a voltage amplifier with an extremely high input impedance.  What is the AC voltage gain of the amplifier?
\end{enumerate}
\begin{center}

\begin{Exercise}
\label{prob_gameshow_2_players}
The circuit below is for a quiz show in which two contestants each try to be the first to hit their button and answer each question.
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item After a question is read, each player hits their button ($PL_1$ or $PL_2$) as fast as they can.  
\item A light (LED1 or LED2) goes on over the contestant that hits their button first.
\item Only the fastest player's light goes on; the slower player's button is then ``locked out'' and has no effect.
\item Before the next question, the host button is pressed to turn off the light and reset the system.
\end{itemize}

\begin{center}
\includegraphics[scale=0.9]{M_problems/applications/gameshow_circuit.pdf}
\end{center}

Initially, all switches are open and both lights are off, so $Q_1=Q_2=0$.  (We'll use ``1'' and ``0'' here instead of specific voltages.)

\begin{enumerate}[nosep, label=(\alph*)]
\item What is the purpose of the three $10\kohm$ resistors to ground?
\item If player 1 hits the button first, what inputs and outputs on U1 and U2 are changed while the switch $PL_1$ is closed? (Hint: of the twelve leads shown, four change values.)
\item What changes when switch $PL_1$ is released (opened)?
\item Player 2 hits their button later than player 1.  What specifically happens to both Q$_2$ and to $\mathrm{\overline{Q}_2}$ while button $PL_2$ is pressed?  Why doesn't Player 2's light go on?
\item It would seem that the circuit could be simplified by removing the two NOT gates and connecting the two lights directly to the Q$_1$ and Q$_2$ outputs.  Why is that not quite the same as what is done above, using the NOT gates?  (Hint: See Lab~\ref{lab_flipflops}, part~\ref{both_set_and_reset}.)
\item The host presses the host button to turn the winning player's light off and reset the system.  Explain how this action affects both U1 and U2.  (Hint: Only one of the two flip-flops is directly affected through its CLOCK and DATA inputs; the other is affected through its SET or RESET input.  See Lab~\ref{lab_flipflops} 
part~\ref{part_synchronous_asynchronous_interference} for an additional hint.)
\end{enumerate}
\end{Exercise}

\begin{Exercise}
\label{prob_derive_555_eqns}
The circuit to the right shows the 555 configured in its astable mode as a square wave generator.
\vspace{-0.1in}
\figuretoright{0.27}{M_problems/applications/astable_555.pdf}{
\begin{enumerate}[nosep, label=(\alph*)]
\item Derive the equations below for the dwell times of the 555 timer in astable mode:
\begin{align*}
\Delta t_\text{high} &= \ln (2) C(R_1 + R_2) \\
\Delta t_\text{low} &= \ln (2) CR_2
\end{align*}
\item Find both the frequency and the duty cycle of the oscillator for $R_1=27\kohm$, $R_2=10\kohm$, and $C=33$~nF.
\item Draw a circuit diagram showing how to use an additional diode to create an oscillator with a frequency of 500~Hz and a duty cycle of 33\%, while maintaining the same value of $C=33$~nF.
\end{enumerate}
}
\end{Exercise}

\begin{Answer}
(b) 930 Hz and 78.7\%.
\end{Answer}

Create an outline for a possible final exam for this course, consisting of about a dozen problems you have seen before.  For each problem, you only need to write where it comes from and give a brief description of what's being asked.  For example,

\begin{description}[labelindent=0.25in, leftmargin=0.5in, nosep]

\item etc.
\end{description}
Your exam should adhere to the following guidelines:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item About 60\% of problems from material before Exam 2, 40\% from after Exam 2.
\item About half of the problems involving circuits to analyze, and about half requiring students to design their own circuits.
\item A mix of problems from different sources: specific lab parts, M problems,  ``possible exam questions'' from labs, and ``circuits to know'' from Appendix~\ref{circuits_to_know}.

\item Do not include any question that appeared exactly on Exam 1 or Exam 2.  (Though it's fine to cover the same topic in a different way, such as requiring the student to design their own circuit rather than analyze a circuit that is given.)
\item Arrange the problems roughly in the order that the topics were covered in class.
\end{itemize}
