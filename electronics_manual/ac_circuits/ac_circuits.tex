\textbf{The Impedance of an Inductor}


The circuit below is essentially a voltage divider between a resistor and an inductor, with a sinusoidal input voltage coming from a buffer amplifier.  
The purpose of the buffer amplifier is to provide a voltage source with a low output impedance, as opposed to your function generator's $R_{\rm int} \approx 600~\Omega$.%  
\footnote{You'll notice a curious addition to our buffer amplifier here: a 2.2~k$\Omega$ ``pulldown'' resistor between the op-amp's output and $V_{\rm EE}$ that would \textit{seem} to have no effect whatsoever, since the amplifier's output still feeds back into $V_-$, ensuring that $V_\text{out}=V_+$.  In fact, the pulldown resistor greatly reduces output noise at high frequencies, eliminating a large voltage spike as the output crosses zero volts.  The reason for the spike is that the LM324 op-amp uses different parts of its internal circuitry for positive and negative output current (i.e., acting as a current ``source'' or current ``sink''); the spike happens during the switch-over between them. The pulldown resistor removes the spike by ensuring that even when  $V_\text{out}<0$, the LM324's output \textit{current} is always positive---with some of it always flowing through $R_\text{pulldown}$ into the negative supply rail at $V_{\rm EE}$.}

Although $V_{\rm AB}$ and $V_{\rm BC}$ are time-varying, for this section of the lab we will measure their RMS values with a DMM---\textit{NOT} the oscilloscope---because it will allow us focus on the circuit's behavior one aspect at a time, as opposed to being confronted with a firehose of unexpected results all at once.

\textbf{But Have We Fully Captured Impedance?}

\item In parts \ref{part_guess_Z_L} and \ref{part_guess_Z_C}, you probably came up with the relationships $Z_L = \omega L$ and $Z_C = 1/ \omega C$.  Those are \textit{close} to being correct, but they don't quite capture everything we want.  Ideally, we'd like for those impedances to follow something like Ohm's law, where $V_L = I Z_L$, and $V_C = I Z_C$.  But if  $I(t) = I_0 \cos(\omega t)$, do the equations $Z_L = \omega L$ and $Z_C = 1/ \omega C$ really give the correct voltages for the inductor and the capacitor?  Why not?  Think about your graphs for parts~\ref{part_RL_graphs} and \ref{part_RC_graphs}, and think carefully about whether the current and voltage are really proportional to each other in each case.
