In the water pipe analogy for circuits, we can think of a power supplies as a pump, a resistor as a constriction in the pipes, and a capacitor as a flexible rubber membrane stretched across an enlarged section of pipe.  Extending the analogy, we can also imagine an inductor as a big, heavy water wheel or turbine that rotates on frictionless bearings as current flows through it; because of its mass, it is hard to start it spinning and hard to stop it.

\item Draw a picture showing the $RL$ circuit you have just built as a system of water pipes, valves, and a pump, and showing the inductor as something big and heavy that spins.  

\item In the picture you have just drawn, the rate of water flow through the inductor changes after the valve is opened or closed.
If the mass of the wheel in your drawing were larger, would the water flow rate\textit{change more quickly} or \textit{change more slowly}?
