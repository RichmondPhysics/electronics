\textbf{Power dissipation in your DMM}

\item In Lab~\ref{lab_input_output_impedance}, you measured that your DMM's internal resistance on the mA~scale is $10\ohm$.  What this means is that the input current literally goes through a $10\ohm$ resistor inside your DMM, and your DMM measures the voltage across that resistor to determine the current.  How much power would be dissipated in that $10\ohm$ resistor if you used it to measure a current of 0.5~amps?  How about 1~amp?

\item To protect your DMM from damage, the mA input jack is protected by a fuse.  Connect your current meter directly to the power supply, with the voltage knobs all the way up, and the current knobs all the way down.  Gradually increase the current of the supply from 0 amps to the maximum possible supply current, reporting your observations in your lab notebook.

\item Your lab station is equipped with a screwdriver and a pair of needle-nose pliers. Use these tools to open the back of your DMM and replace its fuse, which is now blown out.  Spare fuses are next to the $1\ohm$ resistors in your kit.  While your DMM is open, turn it on to check whether the battery icon 
( \raisebox{-0.8ex}{\includegraphics[height=2.8ex]{power/battery_icon.pdf}} )
is displayed on the screen and replace the 9~V battery if needed.

\textbf{Summary Questions:}

\item What is the main difference between the physically large and physically small resistors, and how do you explain why that is?

\item How do your observations compare with the nominal power rating for each resistor?

\item Is the temperature coefficient of each of your resistors positive or negative?  (That is, does the resistance increase or decrease as a function of temperature?) The temperature coefficient may be too small to measure for some of them.  

\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Possible Exam Questions:}

\begin{itemize}
\item Describe a specific set of current and voltage measurements you could make of a resistor to determine whether it had a positive or negative coefficient.  Give an example of numbers for a set of current and voltage measurements that would be consistent with a negative temperature coefficient.

\item Describe what might happen if you dissipate power in a resistor at 5\% greater than its maximum power rating?  What about 150\% of its power rating?  Or 1500\% of its power rating?  Some possibilities may include temporary changes in resistance due to heating, more permanent changes, or expensive insurance settlements.
\end{itemize}
