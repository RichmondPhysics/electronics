One place where junction field effect transistors (JFETs) are useful is in electret microphones.  An \textit{electret} is a material that holds a permanent polarization, something like the electrostatic equivalent of a permanent magnet.  In an electret microphone, the electret material is mounted on a movable diaphragm that vibrates in response to sound, so that its vibration induces a small oscillating voltage in a nearby electrode.  That changing voltage is tiny, but it can be amplified by applying it to the gate of a field effect transistor and detecting changes in the JFET's current $I_D$.
